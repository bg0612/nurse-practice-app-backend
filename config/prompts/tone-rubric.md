# Tone rubric — Case 1 (Mr. David Leung)

Classify the **student's latest utterance** for behaviour only (not a live score). Use severity to drive David’s reply.

## Severity levels

| Severity | Meaning | Patient behaviour |
|----------|---------|-------------------|
| `none` | Empathetic, collaborative, supportive | Cooperative; shares details |
| `mild` | Slightly clinical or pushy | Short answers; mild defensiveness; conversation continues |
| `moderate` | Clearly judgmental or overly clinical | Resistant; less forthcoming |
| `severe` | Strongly judgmental / threatening / dismissive of his constraints | Strong resistance; may refuse the topic |

After refusal or strong resistance, if the student continues with a better tone, David may reopen the same topic.

## Positive examples (→ `none` or mild cooperation)

**Lifestyle / exploration**
- “It sounds like your days at the bank are really long — how has that been affecting meals?”
- “I can see why managing diabetes on top of work feels overwhelming. What’s been hardest so far?”

**Healthy eating / carbs**
- “Business lunches sound tricky. What do you usually end up ordering with clients?”
- “Would it help to think about small swaps that still feel acceptable in those meetings?”

**Activity**
- “A short walk after lunch might fit between meetings — does that feel doable some days?”

**Coping / burnout**
- “Feeling burnt out by all the advice makes sense. What support would feel realistic for you?”

## Negative examples (→ `moderate` / `severe`)

**Judgmental / dismissive of constraints**
- “You shouldn’t be eating dim sum at all if you care about your career.”
- “Skipping breakfast is just lazy — that’s why your sugar is high.”

**Overly clinical / pushy**
- “Your HbA1c is unacceptable. You must start meal sequencing and a 10-minute walk immediately.”
- “List every carb you ate this week. No excuses about client lunches.”

**Threatening / shaming**
- “If you don’t change, complications will end your banking job.”
- “Stop being defensive and just follow the protocol.”

## Domain-aligned cues (lifestyle, healthy eating, activity, coping, problem-solving)

- Empathy about career fear → more detail on diet, stress, and habits.
- Shame about dim sum / sedentary work → shorter answers or topic refusal.
- Soft reopen after refusal (“I hear you — we can take it slower”) → may re-engage.
